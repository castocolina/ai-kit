<domain>
## Phase Boundary

The runner classifies every failure it sees by the channel the host used, from one closed list of classes with a locking test,
and its retry defaults follow the owner's recorded policy: a short first check, then every 10 minutes up to the 4-hour cap, plus
one extra check just after a stated reset time. The catalogue gains the real-run bodies and every host's captured model-missing
wording, plain stdout text counts for a failed attempt, and a router cooldown line is a quota cycle with its reset time on a
confirmed router lane.
</domain>

### The retry policy (SPEC R5; owner decisions 2026-11-16)

- **D-10: The hard tier is a short first check, then every 10 minutes to the cap (owner decision).** `hard_waits_s` default
  `[60, 300]`, new `hard_every_s` (integer, 0 or 60..`capS`, default 600) issues `retry_after_s` repeatedly after the list until the
  cap, then `stopped=cap`; `hard_every_s = 0` keeps the list-end stop, and the shipped pattern is `[600, 900, 1800]` with
  `hard_every_s = 0`. Soft retries, the one session reset and the route rules do not change. `hardWaitFor` takes the periodic
  value; `RETRY_KEYS`, `validateRetryKey`, the `cross-ai: retry ...` line, `RETRY_DEFAULTS`, the rendered `CONFIG.retry`
  (curated-config) and the config documentation gain the key. A wait under 60 s (a configured value) runs inside the call when the
  remaining deadline admits it with the existing 60 s reserve, else it is a 60 s hint: the plan decides how it reuses the soft
  step's admission test. Decision record: `38.1-RETRY-POLICY.md` (evidence: 34 router cooldowns, 24 under 2 minutes, 8 over 4 hours;
  shipped five soft retries in about 13 s, then 600, 900, 1800 s). jev hard_tier_shape low 0.36 (laya shipped), low 0.53 (typesafe
  short_start_then_periodic): the owner decided.
- **D-22: What a client sees from the router's cooldown, read from the OmniRoute source (2026-11-16).** The log line of D-09
  never reaches a client. The client gets 429 with `Retry-After` and `code: "model_cooldown"`, or `ALL_TARGETS_SKIPPED` (429 with
  a hint, else 503), or 503 `all_targets_cooling_down` with `Retry-After`. A cooldown is restored lazily: the lock is deleted when
  `now > until`, so one request right after the stated reset succeeds and no probe is needed. Durations: an exact upstream hint
  (Retry-After, reset text, up to 30 days; header hints bypass the 30-minute cap), a daily quota exhaustion locks until midnight, and
  otherwise exponential backoff from the provider profile (connection bases 5 s OAuth, 3 s API key; model lockout base 120 s, cap
  30 min, off by default per one unverified read). The 24 short and 8 long episodes of the retry evidence fit this (hint-driven or
  midnight locks are the long ones). D-11 therefore reads `Retry-After`, `retry_after` and `reset_seconds` from a real client body.
  A manual clear exists (`DELETE /api/resilience/model-cooldowns`) but is the router owner's, never the runner's. The confirmed
  rows of D-09 match the codes above; the log-wording rows stay only for a runner that is handed router logs.
- **D-11: A stated reset time adds one extra check (owner decision).** Sources: the allowlisted upstream fields (`retry_after`,
  `reset_at`, `retry_after_seconds`, `resetsAt`) and D-09's `reset_after_s`. The check is at the stated time plus 120 s (a window
  clears about 2 minutes after its stated reset), taken only when it is at least 60 s ahead, inside the cap's remaining time and
  earlier than the next periodic check; the periodic checks continue; each stated time is used once per episode (a `reset_check`
  field in the cycle record). A time beyond the cap, in the past or unparseable changes nothing. It never lengthens a wait or lifts a
  stop (the untrusted-data constraint; the SKILL's "upstream fields are never a wait source" sentence is amended to say a stated
  time may add one bounded check and is otherwise data). The report line gains `check=first|periodic|reset|-`; the status line is
  unchanged. jev reset_time_use low 0.52, 0.57 (both extra_check_at_reset). **Needs owner awareness** (upstream text now influences
  one scheduled time; bounded below by 60 s and above by the cap).
- **D-12: A direct lane stays as decided on 2026-11-02 (owner decision).** A quota error on a direct lane stops at once; only
  confirmed router lanes wait on quota; transient (5xx, overloaded, capacity, a cut stream, a timeout attempt) waits on every lane.
  RUN-11's "periodic retries for quota errors" means router lanes only (dated amendment). Evidence recorded: direct vendor limits
  are mostly 5-hour or weekly windows, longer than the cap.
- **D-13: Idle-killed attempts ride the new tier.** Phase 38 D-04 made an idle kill a timed-out transient attempt, so a lane that
  hangs every time now retries about every 10 minutes for up to 4 hours (it used to stop after 55 minutes). Accepted as the
  consequence of one ladder for transient faults; `hard_every_s = 0` or a lower `max_retries` stops it sooner, and the SKILL says
  so. **Needs owner awareness.** Ledger.
- **D-14: The caller text changes, the one-shot rule does not.** Each hint is still one one-shot wait the caller schedules (never a
  repeated job); an episode can now hold about 24 hints. `ai-kit-cross-ai` section 6 and 7, `subprocess-quota-policy.md`, the
  goal-prompt quota rule text pinned in `tests/test_quota_policy_text.py` and the superpowers adapter's "single re-invoke only"
  wording (Phase 41 rewrites that adapter) are listed as places the plan updates or hands over.

### Added 2026-11-16 (free-lane pool study, owner directive, after the R11 amendment; all five decisions are PROVISIONAL and need owner confirmation)

Source: `.planning/research/2026-11-16-opencode-free-models.md` (read 2026-11-16, opencode 2.3.0 on this machine). What it establishes,
with its own labels: the typed error `provider.free-limit` carries an optional `retryAfterMs` taken from the HTTP `Retry-After`
header (verified from opencode's pull request text; live probe on 2026-11-16 gave 429 and about 6 h 44 min); every stated reset in the
sources is under 24 hours (reported, 3.6 h to 23.5 h); the stated reset is unreliable (blocks persisted after it in several reported
issues); the free models appear to share ONE pool per account or network (reported by users, scope unresolved), so another free model
of the same provider is not a fallback for this error; free models work only through the real `opencode` client, so a `FreeTierError`
from a direct call is not a usage limit; the free list is volatile (a removed model is a terminal missing-model error, not quota).
Live check for this edit (2026-11-16): the catalogue row `vendor-free-usage` and the runner's allowlist of upstream fields exist as
SPEC R11 says; `retryAfterMs` is not read today.

- **D-23: One small record per pool in the runner's state directory, keyed by provider and tier.** The pool key is `<provider>/<tier>`
  (`free` for the free pool), so lanes that share a pool block together, which is the evidence-backed reading of the shared pool. The
  record is secret-free JSON (pool, `blocked_until`, `failures`, `basis`, row id, `written_at`), mode 0600, written atomically in the
  state directory beside the cycle and lock records, never in config. It is written only on a `quota` stop whose row carries
  `pool` (the `vendor-free-usage` row) or whose caller names the pool for a lane the owner flagged `usage_limited`; a router quota
  cycle never writes one. Reconciles D-12 (a direct lane still stops at once; the record is for the next run). Pool scope (account,
  IP, workspace, session) is not documented, so the key is a provider-level stand-in and the SPEC says so. **Own reasoning** (no
  jev: it follows the owner's keyed-by-pool directive). Ledger.
- **D-24: Time = stated reset (capped at 24 h) plus the 120 s margin of D-11, else 24 hours.** The owner's margin is 120 s (the
  research note proposed 5 minutes; the owner's figure and D-11 agree, so 120 s). Unstated, zero, negative, non-integer or unparseable
  values give 24 hours. A stated value above 24 hours is capped (the PR text says "up to 24h"; a hostile or wrong header must not
  block a lane for days). `retryAfterMs` is read beside the four allowlisted fields and truncated to whole seconds first (as R5).
  **Owner confirms:** the owner's rule of thumb is "at least the next day or 24 hours"; the evidence says a stated reset is shorter and
  the same-day retry it allows is valuable, so the stated time is used first (Recommended, evidence-led) and the 24-hour rule applies
  as the default and as the repeat-failure floor; the alternative is a flat 24 hours always (simpler, wastes the same-day window).
  **Own reasoning.** Ledger.
- **D-25: A second consecutive failure after the time floors the block at 24 hours (owner rule, provisional).** `failures` counts
  failures after the time with no ok attempt of the pool between; the second and every later one use the larger of the stated value
  and 24 hours. A failure inside an unexpired window (a caller that ran the lane anyway) keeps `failures` and takes the later time.
  An ok attempt on any lane of the pool deletes the record. The research note also suggests a user notice that the pool may be stuck
  after two failures and a paid route: the runner prints the count in the read option and the notice is the caller's (Phases 40 and 41),
  so no new runner text. **Owner confirms** the rule and whether a third failure should extend further (the note suggests another 24 h;
  here every later failure already floors at 24 h). **Own reasoning.** Ledger.
- **D-26: A read-only query and a manual clear; the runner never decides.** `--blocked-status <pool>` prints `blocked_until` and
  `failures` and spawns nothing; `--blocked-clear <pool>` deletes the record (the owner's re-enable). A missing, unreadable or
  malformed record reads as no record (fail open: one extra call is cheaper than a lane blocked forever by a corrupt file). Selection
  and fallback stay with the callers (Phase 40 skips a blocked lane and uses `lane_fallback`; Phase 41 does the same for execute
  lanes), consistent with 38.1's rule that the runner never switches harness, provider or model. The option names are the plan's; they
  join Phase 37's vocabulary and `--describe`. **Own reasoning.** Ledger.
- **D-27: What is not a usage limit.** `FreeTierError`, `MissingSessionID` and the "newer version required" gate are client rules: the
  runner always runs through the real `opencode` client, so they mean a misconfigured lane. They classify as `bad_request` (terminal,
  exit 69, never retried, no record), rows scoped to the opencode runtime and labeled `unverified` (reported in a pinned maintainer post
  and issues; no body captured here). A removed or deprecated free model stays `bad_model` through the captured `provider.no-route`
  `Model unavailable:` row, terminal, no record; that this event is what 2.3.0 prints for a deprecated-but-listed model is unverified
  (the research note found only an opaque `UnknownError` on 1.18.25). Reconciles with a catalogue narrowness rule (D-04: no bare
  `unavailable` row). **Own reasoning.** Ledger.

**Unverified items from the research note (labeled; none is a requirement until captured):**
- The reset rule (fixed boundary in some timezone, or rolling window) and the key (IP, account, workspace, session): sources conflict,
  maintainers have not stated it; a local experiment (record each `provider.free-limit` time, `retryAfterMs` and computed reset over
  several days) would settle it for this machine.
- Whether opencode 2.3.0 exits non-zero after `provider.free-limit` in `--format json`, and which events precede it: not tested (it
  would spend quota). The plan must not depend on the exit code; the classifier reads the event, and the exit-code path is checked
  with a mocked 429.
- What headless 2.3.0 prints for a removed or deprecated free model (type name and status): no source found.
- Whether the `opencode` and `opencode-go` free ids share one pool: not documented. The key `<provider>/free` keeps them apart until
  evidence says otherwise, which may under-block; the owner can edit the recorded pool string (Phase 36).
